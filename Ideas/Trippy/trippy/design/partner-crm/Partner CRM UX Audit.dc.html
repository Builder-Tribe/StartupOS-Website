03 — Critical fixes

Top 5 P0 issues — detailed fix spec

1
Add first-run onboarding wizard — partners must not land on a blank dashboard
P0PartnerApp.tsx · post-signup routing · missing screen
After signup, a new partner has just one job: publish their first trip. Instead, they land on a blank dashboard with a single "Create Trip" button. There is no welcome, no guidance, no progress indicator, and no answer to "what do I do first?" This is the primary abandonment point. Partners who have only ever used WhatsApp + Google Forms need a completely guided first-trip experience.
Fix: Detect first login via admin.firstLogin === true or org.trips === 0. Route to an onboarding wizard (see Section 5) instead of the blank dashboard. Wizard: Welcome → Trip basics → Pricing → Payment → Publish. Each step is completable in under 2 minutes. Step 1 creates a draft trip immediately so progress is never lost. A "Continue setup" banner persists on the dashboard until the first trip is published.
2
Add section navigation + completion progress to the trip editor
P0TripEditor.tsx · single-scroll form · missing navigation
The trip editor is currently a single unbroken scroll of 30+ fields across 4 implicit sections (Basic Info, Pricing, Payment, Itinerary). There is no way to jump between sections, no indication of what's complete vs missing, and no "minimum viable trip" guidance. A partner who fills in the title, destination, and dates has no idea they still need 20 more things before publishing. This single issue is responsible for the majority of trip-builder abandonment.
Fix: Add a left sidebar to the trip editor (as designed in Screen 2a of the Partner CRM + Admin Redesign file) showing 6 sections as a checklist: Basic info → Itinerary → Pricing → Inclusions → Payment link → Media. Each section shows ✓ / ✕ / active state. A completion percentage bar shows overall progress. The sticky action bar shows "X of 6 sections complete" + "Publish" button (disabled until all required sections are done). This transforms the editor from a form-dump into a guided workflow.
3
Payment link field needs full guidance — it's the #1 publish blocker
P0TripEditor.tsx · payment link section · missing guidance
The payment link is required for publishing, but many partners don't have one. The current UX is: one input field, one validation error if https:// is missing. A partner who uses Google Forms, WhatsApp Business, or manual bank transfers doesn't know what to enter here. They will either enter an invalid URL, enter nothing and be blocked from publishing, or abandon the trip entirely.
Fix: Expand the payment link section into a guidance card: (1) "What is this?" explainer: "Paste the link where travellers pay you — Razorpay, Instamojo, your website, or any https:// page." (2) Quick-start buttons: [Razorpay] [Instamojo] [My website] — each opens a helper tooltip with how to get the link. (3) Accept pattern: show a green ✓ + "Looks good" when a valid https link is entered. (4) If no payment system: "No online payment yet? Create a free Razorpay link in 5 minutes →" (external link). (5) Keep the hard requirement — trip can't go live without it — but make reaching that requirement possible for non-technical partners.
4
Add sidebar navigation — the portal needs a clear information architecture
P0PartnerApp.tsx · crm-topbar · no sidebar
The current portal has two destinations: Dashboard and TripEditor. The topbar shows org name + "Consumer site ↗" + admin name + log out. There is no way to access org settings, team management, or analytics. As the portal grows (Phase 2: Razorpay, analytics, SaaS tiers), bolting features onto a topbar will break. The partner has no mental model of what the portal contains.
Fix: Add a persistent left sidebar (220px, white, border-right) with sections: My trips · Analytics (coming soon, greyed) · Organisation · Team · Payment links. The org name + emoji are shown at the top of the sidebar (the multi-tenant identity anchor — always visible). "Trippy Partner Portal" sub-label below. Phase 2 features shown as greyed "Coming soon" items — not hidden, but clearly not clickable. This sets the expectation for what the portal will become while keeping Phase 1 clean.
5
Preview must open in a new tab and clearly explain how to return to editing
P0Preview.tsx · `/partner/trips/:id/preview` route
Currently, clicking "Preview" in the trip editor navigates to the preview route in the same tab. The partner must use the browser's back button to return to editing — no in-preview CTA for this. On mobile/tablet, this is particularly disorienting. The preview ribbon says nothing about how to return.
Fix: Change Preview link to target="_blank" — open in a new tab. This means the partner keeps the editor open and can switch between the consumer view and the editing view naturally. Update the preview ribbon to: "🔍 You're seeing this trip as a traveller would. Close this tab to return to editing." Also add a persistent "Edit this trip" button in the top-right of the preview. The partner should never be stranded.
04 — Revamp briefs

5 core partner jobs

1
Sign up & org setup
Happy path
Land on /partner/login → read value prop ("Reach 12,000+ solo travellers — list your trips free") → click "Create account" → enter org name + name + email + password → create account → land in onboarding wizard (not blank dashboard).
Edge cases
Email already registered: "An account with this email exists — sign in instead?" with switch-to-login CTA. Org name blank: "What's your community called?" inline nudge. Weak password: Show strength indicator + "At least 8 characters, include a number."
Key components
PartnerLoginCard · ValuePropRow · OrgNameInput · PasswordStrengthBar · WelcomeModal (post-signup)
Improvements
Add a 3-bullet value prop above the form: "✓ 12,000+ solo travellers searching your destination · ✓ Zero commission · ✓ No payment integration — use your existing link." Add a "See how it works" link (opens a 30-second demo modal). Forgot password flow.
2
Dashboard — daily ops home base
Happy path
Log in → see dashboard → immediately see what needs action (drafts needing completion, upcoming trips in 30 days) → tap into the trip needing attention → fix it → publish or prepare.
Edge cases
First login (no trips): Onboarding wizard (not blank dashboard). All trips live, nothing to do: Show "Your trips are all set 🎉" + "Create a new trip for the next season." Trip expiring soon (start date <7 days): Highlight in amber with "Upcoming in 5 days."
Key components
ActionRequired card · KPITileRow · TripTable (sortable) · TripHealthIndicator · BookingClicksBar · UpcomingAlert
Improvements
Surface "drafts needing action" first (above the full table). Show booking click count inline in the trip table. Add a "Continue setup →" sticky banner if first trip isn't published yet.
3
Trip builder — the most critical flow
Happy path
Click "Create trip" → wizard step 1: trip name + destination + dates (autosave creates draft) → step 2: itinerary (add days one by one) → step 3: pricing + inclusions → step 4: payment link (guided) → step 5: cover photo → review checklist → click "Go live" → confirm → published → success screen with consumer-facing URL to share.
Edge cases
Partner leaves mid-build: Draft is saved automatically. Dashboard shows "Continue your Spiti trip →" banner. Validation fails on publish: Errors shown as checklist items with direct jump links — not a wall of red text. No payment link: Guided creation flow, not a hard block with no escape. Trip dates in the past: Inline warning "These dates have passed — is this trip still upcoming?"
Key components
SectionNav · CompletionBar · FieldWithHelpText · ItineraryDayCard · PaymentLinkGuide · PublishChecklist · PublishSuccessScreen
Improvements
Section navigation sidebar with completion checkmarks. Minimum viable publish guidance: "You need at least: title, destination, dates, price, and payment link to go live." Trip duplication: "Copy from a previous trip" to avoid re-entering recurring info. Preview always accessible from any step via "Preview" button (new tab).
4
Trip lifecycle management
Happy path
Draft → validate (auto on publish attempt) → live → upcoming (when start date <30 days) → completed (after end date) → optional: archived.
Edge cases
Publish fails validation: Each error links directly to the field that needs fixing. Unpublish a live trip: Confirm dialog: "Unpublishing removes this trip from Trippy search. Travellers who've already clicked Book Now won't be affected." Trip completed automatically: System moves to completed after end date — email notification to partner. Admin suspends trip: Partner sees a "This trip is suspended — contact Trippy support" banner on the editor.
Key components
StatusBadge (7 states) · TransitionButton (state-aware) · ConfirmUnpublishModal · SuspendedBanner · CompletedBanner
Improvements
Make status transitions explicit with a visible "lifecycle flow" tooltip: "Draft → Live → Upcoming → Completed." Partners often don't understand why their trip shows as "upcoming" when they expect it to be "live."
5
Booking click tracking & analytics
Happy path
Partner opens dashboard → sees booking click count per trip inline in the table → clicks a trip to see day-by-day click trend → understands "Spiti Valley Circuit gets 15 clicks/day."
Edge cases
0 clicks on a live trip: "No clicks yet — your trip has been live for 3 days. Tips for more visibility →" (Phase 1 placeholder). Very high clicks but no bookings (Phase 2): "High interest — consider adding a direct booking option." (Phase 2 teaser).
Key components
BookingClickCount (inline in table) · ClickTrendMini (sparkline in trip detail) · PhaseTeaser ("Coming soon: conversion analytics")
Improvements
Phase 1: Show click count inline in the dashboard trip table — no separate analytics tab needed. Phase 2 teaser: A greyed "Analytics" nav item with "Coming soon — see conversion rates, lead sources, booking funnel." Never show empty analytics UI — only the teaser.

06 — Dashboard design

What the partner sees on every login

Information hierarchy: what needs action (drafts, upcoming) comes first. What is informational (completed, all-time stats) comes last. The partner should never have to hunt for what to do next.

Layout recommendation — top to bottom
Zone 1 — Urgency (always first): If drafts exist → "1 trip needs attention before it can go live" card with direct edit link. If upcoming trips in <7 days → "Spiti Valley Circuit leaves in 4 days" amber banner. If first-login → onboarding wizard. These are conditional — disappear when resolved.
Zone 2 — 4 KPI metrics (actionable): Total trips · Live now · Book Now clicks (30d) · Upcoming trips. "Completed" and "Draft" counts are secondary and moved to a filters strip above the table — not KPI tiles. The 4 KPI tiles chosen are all forward-looking or actionable, not historical.
Zone 3 — Trip table (sorted by urgency): Default sort: upcoming trips first, then live, then draft (needing action), then completed. Each row shows: trip name · destination · dates · status badge · booking clicks bar · actions. Trip rows glow amber if start date <7 days. Rows with missing payment link show a ⚠ icon.
Zone 4 — Phase 2 teaser (bottom, unobtrusive): A single quiet card: "Coming soon: direct booking + conversion analytics." Never shows as an empty locked UI — just a small teaser card at the bottom.
The 4 recommended KPI metrics and why
3
Live trips
The partner's primary output. Not "total trips" — that inflates with drafts. "Live" is what matters.
148
Book Now clicks (30d)
The clearest proxy for revenue interest. Partners need to know their trips are working. No other metric is as motivating.
1
Upcoming departures
Creates urgency. A trip leaving in 6 days needs final prep. This number should drive weekly ops behaviour.
1
Drafts needing action
Makes incomplete drafts visible. "Draft" implies "not earning." This metric creates internal pressure to finish and publish.
Trip table column priority
1Trip name (link to editor)
2Dates + destination
3Status badge (colour-coded)
4Booking clicks (mini bar chart)
5Actions (Edit · Preview · Publish/Unpublish · Delete)
Remove: "Updated at" column. Not actionable — replaced by the ⚠ indicator on rows needing attention.

09 — Design checklist

20-point pre-handoff validation for partner portal screens

Activation (4 checks)
1
First-login state is handled. New partner with 0 trips never sees a blank dashboard. Onboarding wizard launches automatically.
2
"Continue setup" banner persists on the dashboard until the first trip is published. Partners should always know how to pick up where they left off.
3
Time-to-first-publish under 10 minutes for a partner who has their trip info ready. Validated by testing with a real operator who has never used the portal.
4
Publish success screen shows the consumer-facing URL and a "Copy link to share" button. Partner's first live moment must feel celebratory.
Trip builder (5 checks)
5
Autosave indicator always visible in the editor — never hidden off-screen. Partner should never wonder if their work is saved.
6
Completion percentage visible in the section nav sidebar. Partner knows "4 of 6 sections done" at a glance.
7
Payment link field has full guidance — not just a text input. Includes explainer, examples, and "help me create one" link for non-technical partners.
8
Publish validation errors are specific and actionable. "Your trip is missing a payment link" with a direct jump link — never "Validation error 422."
9
Preview opens in a new tab and includes a clear "close this tab to return to editing" instruction. Partner must never be stranded in preview with no way back.
Multi-tenancy & trust (3 checks)
10
Org name is always visible in the sidebar and topbar. A partner who manages multiple orgs (Phase 2) must always know which org they're in.
11
No data cross-contamination. Every API call is scoped to the authenticated org. The UI must never show trip names, analytics, or team members from another org — even as empty/loading states.
12
Suspended state is unambiguous. A suspended trip shows a red banner with contact instructions. The partner knows exactly what happened and what to do — not a confusing 403 error.
Tablet UX (4 checks)
13
Touch targets ≥44px on all buttons, day reorder controls, and itinerary action buttons. Partners often work on-site with tablets where precision is lower.
14
Sidebar collapses to icons-only on tablet (<900px) to preserve editor space. Full label visible on hover/tap.
15
Itinerary day reorder works on tablet. Up/down arrows are minimum 44px tap targets. Future: drag-to-reorder with long-press gesture.
16
Sticky action bar is always accessible on tablet — not hidden behind keyboard or cut off by viewport height changes.
Copy & tone (4 checks)
17
No jargon. "Your trips" not "listing pipeline." "Go live" not "publish to production." "Book Now clicks" not "outbound CTR." Every label tested with a non-technical partner.
18
Help text is contextual, not generic. Every input field has a real example (not a placeholder like "enter text here"). The example must match a real Indian travel scenario.
19
Phase 2 features are teased, not faked. "Coming soon: direct booking" as a small footer card. Never shown as a broken/empty/locked UI section that confuses the partner.
20
Error states are warm, not punishing. "Your trip needs a payment link before it can go live — here's how to add one" not "Publishing failed. Required field missing." The partner should feel supported, not judged.
